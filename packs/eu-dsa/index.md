## CHAPTER I — GENERAL PROVISIONS
- art_001.md | Article 1 | Subject matter | This part defines the purpose and scope of the Digital Services Act, including its goals and the rules it establishes for online intermediary services.
- art_002.md | Article 2 | Scope | This part defines the scope of the Digital Services Act, including where it applies and which services or laws it does not affect.
- art_003.md | Article 3 | Definitions | This part defines key terms used throughout the Digital Services Act, such as 'online platform,' 'illegal content,' and 'intermediary service'.
## CHAPTER II — LIABILITY OF PROVIDERS OF INTERMEDIARY SERVICES
- art_004.md | Article 4 | ‘Mere conduit’ | This part explains when online service providers are not held legally responsible for content they merely transmit or provide access to without altering it.
- art_005.md | Article 5 | ‘Caching’ | This part explains the rules for temporary storage of online content to improve efficiency or security, known as caching.
- art_006.md | Article 6 | Hosting | This part explains the conditions under which hosting service providers are not liable for illegal content stored at the request of users.
- art_007.md | Article 7 | Voluntary own-initiative investigations and legal compliance | This part explains that conducting voluntary investigations or compliance measures does not disqualify providers from liability exemptions under the Digital Services Act.
- art_008.md | Article 8 | No general monitoring or active fact-finding obligations | This part states that providers of intermediary services have no general obligation to monitor the information they transmit or store, or to actively seek facts or circumstances indicating illegal activity.
- art_009.md | Article 9 | Orders to act against illegal content | This part sets out the rules for orders from national judicial or administrative authorities to providers of intermediary services to act against illegal content, including what such orders must contain and how providers must respond.
- art_010.md | Article 10 | Orders to provide information | This part explains the rules for how authorities can request information about specific users from online service providers and how those providers must respond.
## CHAPTER III — DUE DILIGENCE OBLIGATIONS FOR A TRANSPARENT AND SAFE ONLINE ENVIRONMENT

- art_012.md | Article 12 | Points of contact for recipients of the service | This part explains the requirement for providers of intermediary services to establish a single, easily accessible point of contact for recipients to communicate with them directly.

- art_018.md | Article 18 | Notification of suspicions of criminal offences | This part explains when and how hosting service providers must report suspicions of serious criminal offences to authorities under the Digital Services Act.

- art_022.md | Article 22 | Trusted flaggers | This part covers the rules for trusted flaggers, including how they are designated, their reporting obligations, and the process for suspending or revoking their status.

- art_024.md | Article 24 | Transparency reporting obligations for providers of online platforms | This part covers the transparency reporting requirements for online platforms, including dispute resolution data, suspension statistics, and monthly active user reporting.
- art_025.md | Article 25 | Online interface design and organisation | This section covers rules on how online platforms must design their interfaces to avoid manipulation or distortion of user decisions.
- art_026.md | Article 26 | Advertising on online platforms | This part covers the rules for displaying advertisements on online platforms, including transparency requirements and restrictions on profiling using sensitive personal data.
- art_027.md | Article 27 | Recommender system transparency | This part explains the transparency requirements for recommender systems under the Digital Services Act, including how platforms must disclose and allow modification of key parameters.
- art_028.md | Article 28 | Online protection of minors | This part covers the rules for online platforms to protect minors, including privacy measures and restrictions on targeted advertising.
### SECTION 4 — Additional provisions applicable to providers of online platforms allowing consumers to conclude distance contracts with traders

- art_031.md | Article 31 | Compliance by design | This part explains how online platforms must design their interfaces to help traders meet legal requirements for selling products or services to consumers in the EU.
- art_032.md | Article 32 | Right to information | This part explains the obligations of online platforms to inform consumers about illegal products or services they may have purchased.
### SECTION 5 — Additional obligations for providers of very large online platforms and of very large online search engines to manage systemic risks

- art_035.md | Article 35 | Mitigation of risks | This part covers the measures providers of very large online platforms and search engines must take to mitigate systemic risks, such as adapting design, terms, content moderation, algorithms and advertising, cooperating with trusted flaggers and other providers, protecting minors and labelling generated content, plus the Board's reports and the Commission's guidelines on these measures.

- art_037.md | Article 37 | Independent audit | This part covers the rules for independent audits of very large online platforms and search engines to ensure compliance with legal obligations and commitments.
- art_038.md | Article 38 | Recommender systems | This part explains the rules for recommender systems under the Digital Services Act, requiring options that do not rely on profiling.
- art_039.md | Article 39 | Additional online advertising transparency | This part covers the requirements for very large online platforms and search engines to create and maintain a public, searchable repository of online advertisements, including specific details about the ads and their targeting.
- art_040.md | Article 40 | Data access and scrutiny | This section outlines rules for data access and scrutiny by regulators and vetted researchers under the Digital Services Act.
- art_041.md | Article 41 | Compliance function | This part outlines the requirements for establishing and maintaining an independent compliance function within very large online platforms or search engines under the Digital Services Act.
- art_042.md | Article 42 | Transparency reporting obligations | This section outlines the detailed transparency reporting requirements for very large online platforms and search engines under the Digital Services Act.
- art_043.md | Article 43 | Supervisory fee | This part explains how the European Commission charges and calculates annual supervisory fees for very large online platforms and search engines under the Digital Services Act.

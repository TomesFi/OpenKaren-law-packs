# General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

## Recitals
- rec_001-010.md | Recitals 1–10 | Preamble | This section introduces the purpose and context of the GDPR, explaining its fundamental rights basis and the need for a unified data protection framework across the EU.
- rec_011-020.md | Recitals 11–20 | Preamble | This part covers why the GDPR takes the form of a regulation, that it protects natural persons and not legal persons such as companies, its technology neutrality, the processing it excludes (purely personal or household activity, national security, and law enforcement under Directive (EU) 2016/680), and how it treats EU institutions and courts acting in their judicial capacity.
- rec_021-030.md | Recitals 21–30 | Preamble | This part covers the scope and application of GDPR, including territorial reach, pseudonymisation, and the treatment of deceased persons' data.
- rec_031-040.md | Recitals 31–40 | Preamble | This section covers definitions, legal bases for processing, transparency requirements, and special protections for children under GDPR.
- rec_041-050.md | Recitals 41–50 | Preamble | This section explains the legal bases for processing personal data under GDPR, including consent, contracts, legal obligations, legitimate interests, and public interest.
- rec_051-060.md | Recitals 51–60 | Preamble | This part explains the rules for processing sensitive personal data, including when exceptions apply and how transparency and data subject rights are ensured.
- rec_061-070.md | Recitals 61–70 | Preamble | This part covers the rights of data subjects regarding access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection to processing under GDPR.
- rec_071-080.md | Recitals 71–80 | Preamble | This section explains rules on automated decision-making, profiling, risk assessments, and the responsibilities of data controllers under GDPR.
- rec_081-090.md | Recitals 81–90 | Preamble | This part covers the obligations of controllers and processors regarding data security, risk assessments, and breach notifications under GDPR.
- rec_091-100.md | Recitals 91–100 | Preamble | This part covers when a data protection impact assessment is required and how it should be conducted, including consultation with supervisory authorities.
- rec_101-110.md | Recitals 101–110 | Preamble | This part covers rules for transferring personal data from the EU to countries outside the EU or international organizations, including adequacy decisions and safeguards when such protections are not guaranteed.
- rec_111-120.md | Recitals 111–120 | Preamble | This section outlines specific conditions and exceptions for transferring personal data outside the EU, including grounds like consent, public interest, and vital interests, as well as rules for supervisory authorities and international cooperation.
- rec_121-130.md | Recitals 121–130 | Preamble | This part covers the roles, powers, and cooperation of supervisory authorities under GDPR, including lead authority responsibilities and enforcement mechanisms.
- rec_131-140.md | Recitals 131–140 | Preamble | This part covers the roles, cooperation, and enforcement mechanisms of supervisory authorities under GDPR, including lead authority responsibilities, mutual assistance, and the consistency mechanism.
- rec_141-150.md | Recitals 141–150 | Preamble | This part covers the rights of data subjects to lodge complaints, seek judicial remedies, and claim compensation, as well as the enforcement mechanisms including administrative fines and penalties under GDPR.
- rec_151-160.md | Recitals 151–160 | Preamble | This section covers how GDPR balances data protection with national legal traditions, freedom of expression, public access to documents, employment data processing, and research or archiving purposes.
- rec_161-170.md | Recitals 161–170 | Preamble | This part covers rules on consent for clinical trials, processing data for statistical purposes, professional secrecy, and the powers of supervisory authorities under GDPR.
- rec_171-173.md | Recitals 171–173 | Preamble | This part covers the transition from the old data protection rules to the new GDPR, including repeals, ongoing processing, and the relationship with other EU laws.
## CHAPTER I — General provisions
- art_001.md | Article 1 | Subject-matter and objectives | This part covers the purpose and scope of the GDPR, including its focus on protecting personal data and ensuring its free movement within the EU.
- art_002.md | Article 2 | Material scope | This section defines the types of data processing activities and entities to which GDPR applies or does not apply.
- art_003.md | Article 3 | Territorial scope | This section defines when the GDPR applies based on where data processing occurs or where individuals are located.
- art_004.md | Article 4 | Definitions | This part defines key terms and concepts used throughout the GDPR regulation.
## CHAPTER II — Principles
- art_005.md | Article 5 | Principles relating to processing of personal data | This part covers the core principles that organizations must follow when processing personal data under GDPR.
- art_006.md | Article 6 | Lawfulness of processing | This part covers the legal bases under which personal data can be processed under GDPR, including consent, contracts, legal obligations, and legitimate interests.
- art_007.md | Article 7 | Conditions for consent | This part explains the rules for obtaining and managing consent under GDPR, including how it must be given, withdrawn, and demonstrated.
- art_008.md | Article 8 | Conditions applicable to child's consent in relation to information society services | This part explains the rules for when a child can consent to their personal data being used online, including age requirements and how to verify parental consent.
- art_009.md | Article 9 | Processing of special categories of personal data | This section explains the rules and exceptions for processing sensitive personal data under GDPR Article 9.
- art_010.md | Article 10 | Processing of personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences | This section explains the rules for handling personal data about criminal convictions and offences under GDPR.
- art_011.md | Article 11 | Processing which does not require identification | This part explains when a data controller does not need to identify a person to process their data under GDPR.
## CHAPTER III — Rights of the data subject
### Section 1 — Transparency and modalities
- art_012.md | Article 12 | Transparent information, communication and modalities for the exercise of the rights of the data subject | This part explains how organizations must provide clear, accessible information to individuals about their data processing and how they should handle requests related to data rights.
### Section 2 — Information and access to personal data
- art_013.md | Article 13 | Information to be provided where personal data are collected from the data subject | This part covers the specific information that must be provided to individuals when their personal data is collected directly from them under GDPR Article 13.
- art_014.md | Article 14 | Information to be provided where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject | This part explains what information controllers must provide to individuals when their personal data is collected from sources other than themselves.
- art_015.md | Article 15 | Right of access by the data subject | This part explains the data subject's right to access their personal data and related details from the controller under GDPR Article 15.
### Section 3 — Rectification and erasure
- art_016.md | Article 16 | Right to rectification | This section explains the right of individuals to correct or complete their personal data held by organizations.
- art_017.md | Article 17 | Right to erasure (‘right to be forgotten’) | This section explains when individuals can request the deletion of their personal data and the conditions under which controllers must comply.
- art_018.md | Article 18 | Right to restriction of processing | This part explains when individuals can request to limit how their personal data is used and what happens when such a restriction is in place.
- art_019.md | Article 19 | Notification obligation regarding rectification or erasure of personal data or restriction of processing | This part explains when and how organizations must notify others about changes to personal data under GDPR Article 19.
- art_020.md | Article 20 | Right to data portability | This section explains the right of individuals to transfer their personal data between services under specific conditions.
### Section 4 — Right to object and automated individual decision-making
- art_021.md | Article 21 | Right to object | This part explains the data subject's right to object to the processing of their personal data under GDPR, including for direct marketing and scientific research.
- art_022.md | Article 22 | Automated individual decision-making, including profiling | This part explains when automated decisions affecting individuals are allowed under GDPR and the safeguards required.
### Section 5 — Restrictions
- art_023.md | Article 23 | Restrictions | This section explains when and how EU or Member State laws can limit data subject rights under GDPR for specific public interests or protections.
